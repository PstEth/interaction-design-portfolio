Project 01 · 2026

AI Gym Mirror

Making workout feedback visible, immediate, and actionable.

Role
Interaction Designer / UI
Duration
12 weeks
Tools
Figma · Python · Computer Vision
Team
4 — design + eng
Platform
Web · Camera

Live tracking. The skeleton turns red when form breaks; a single cue appears on the camera.

Problem

Training without a trainer.

People can follow a workout, but they cannot see their own form. Mistakes stay invisible until they become pain, plateau, or injury. Feedback — if it exists — arrives after the set is already over.

User→ Exercise→ Incorrect form→ No immediate feedback→ Risk / inefficient training

Design question

How might we give people useful workout feedback without requiring a personal trainer?

Research → Insight

The interface must stay secondary to the body.

01

Users miss subtle posture errors

The body is a poor sensor for small deviations. Feedback has to make the invisible visible — without turning the gym into a dashboard.

02

Feedback belongs in the movement

Post-set summaries arrive too late. Correction only works if it happens during the rep, in the same tempo as the exercise.

03

Too much data breaks flow

Counting angles and showing skeletons competes with the workout. The system should speak in glances, not paragraphs.

04

UI is a supporting actor

The primary object is the body in space. Screens, overlays, and voice must recede the moment form is correct.

User flow

From camera setup to set review.

Open app→ Select exercise→ Position camera→ Start→ AI tracks→ Detects form→ Gives feedback→ Complete set→ Review

Low-fidelity wireframes

Seven screens. Grayscale on purpose.

The system is designed around the live camera state, not a content feed. Each screen exists to get the user into — or out of — physical movement with as little interface as possible.

Interaction system

How the interface answers the body.

Correct form

Quiet confirmation. No extra UI. A thin edge cue or haptic tick. The workout continues.

Incorrect form

One cue, in the movement: “Shoulder is swinging forward. Keep your elbow fixed at your side.” The skeleton goes red. It disappears when the body corrects.

Rep completed

Count increments. No celebration. Rhythm stays with the user.

Set completed

Camera recedes. Summary appears only after motion stops.

Tracking lost

Soft pause. Re-alignment silhouette. Never a hard error screen mid-set.

High-fidelity interface

From wireframe to a product you can stand in front of.

Incorrect form. Pose is drawn in red. The right rail holds reps, score, and one instruction — not a dashboard of angles.

When form is quiet, the overlay recedes to white. History appears only after the set — date, exercise, reps, load, duration.

System screens built from the same language.

Home, exercise choice, and set complete — generated to match the live product: black canvas, pixel labels, camera first.

Outcome

A camera-based training companion that makes form visible in real time, keeps the interface out of the way, and turns a solo workout into a guided one — without a trainer in the room.

Reflection

Lesson 01

Feedback is a timing problem

The valuable design decision was not what to show, but when to show it. Late information is decoration.

Lesson 02

Absence is a state

Correct form needed a designed quiet — not a green check that never leaves the screen.

Lesson 03

Sensing is part of UX

Camera placement, lighting, and body distance are interaction design, not engineering leftovers.
